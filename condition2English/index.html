Information about the case

The police received a report of a possible burglary in a home office on Jun 25th, at around 21.50 hours. The report was filed by Hendrik van Kampen. He stated that he frequently walks the dog of his daughter, Jolanda, when she is busy with her work as an investigative journalist. He also has a key to her house to be able to do so. Hendrik lives a couple of blocks away from Jolanda. Jolanda had asked him earlier that day if he could walk the dog, as she was busy at work. Hendrik had come by and, when he had wanted to grab a toy from the garden, he had seen that the office in the garden had been ransacked. Jolanda was typically a tidy person, especially as she sometimes used the office to receive guests and interview people. Hendrik had tried calling his daughter, but he could not get a hold of her. That happened occasionally when she was working on an important story. Jolanda had told Hendrik before that she was working on a story about recent shooting incidents. She had recently found a criminal source that was willing to talk to her about these shootings. The office is located in the back of the garden of Jolanda and is only accessible via her garden. The garden is fenced off. There is an alley behind the office that is poorly lit and exclusively provides access to the gardens in Jolanda's street. Both neighbours turn out to be away on holiday. There are no security cameras that record the garden or the alley behind it either. Hendrik further explained that he knew that Jolanda used the office to work in and to store part of her belongings. Hendrik had not been in the office for a while, so he could not tell the police whether items were missing or left behind. Only Jolanda and Mark, her partner, have keys to the office. Mark had gone away on vacation with friends a few days ago. Hendrik did not know where the keys of Mark and Jolanda are. He could only say that the bowl that usually contains keys when Hendrik visits his daughter was now empty. Hendrik noticed nothing else that was out of the ordinary inside the house. There are also no clear signs of a forced entry visible in the house or the garden nor other signs of disturbance. The door of the office turns out to be unlocked when the police officers arrive. The uniformed officers have not entered the office.
